Can machine learning improve daily retail demand forecasts over a seasonal baseline on a public benchmark?

31.03%Model WAPE
44.4%Seasonal baseline WAPE
↘

What the result means: a ≈30.1% relative reduction in WAPE, calculated from 31.03% versus the 44.4% seasonal baseline, on the M5 Walmart dataset. The project reports statistically significant improvement across 7 of 7 stores tested.

Approach
LightGBM using historical sales and temporal, rolling, price and promotion features.
Validation
Temporal holdout, date-block bootstrap and paired comparison against a seasonal baseline.
Limitations
Benchmark performance is technical evidence, not proof of lower waste, higher profit or customer impact. Commercial validation remains open.

02 / VALIDATION ENGINEERING

A result is only
the beginning.

A useful forecast needs more than a strong score. The validation process asks whether the result holds up over time, how uncertain it is, and whether it matters outside the metric.

STAGE 01 / 05 METHOD OVERVIEW

Start with a comparison.

A model score is hard to interpret in isolation. Compare it with a clearly defined baseline on aligned evaluation data before describing an improvement.

A better score is an observation — not, by itself, proof of generalization or business value.

These methods help investigate specific failure modes. They do not automatically eliminate all bias or guarantee that a model will generalize.

EventHorizon-AI grew from hands-on exploration of machine learning into an independent research and engineering project. The work spans forecasting models, evaluation tooling, and the harder question of what results do — and do not — establish.

Built independently by Lucas, 14, in Brazil, the project is a place to learn in public, share useful tools, and document negative results as carefully as positive ones.
