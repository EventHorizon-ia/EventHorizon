SYSTEM 01 Technical benchmark

EventHorizon Demand

Can machine learning improve daily retail demand forecasts over a seasonal baseline on a public benchmark?

31.03%Model WAPE
44.4%Seasonal baseline WAPE
↘

What the result means: a ≈30.1% relative reduction in WAPE, calculated from 31.03% versus the 44.4% seasonal baseline, on the M5 Walmart dataset. The improvement was observed in all 7 stores evaluated, with statistical significance reported in the project documentation. This is a benchmark result: no commercial pilots have been executed, and practical value in real operations remains under investigation.

Approach
LightGBM trained on M5 Walmart daily sales, using sales history plus the temporal, rolling, price and promotion features available in the dataset.
Validation
Temporal holdout, date-block bootstrap and paired comparison against a seasonal baseline.
Limitations
Benchmark performance is technical evidence, not proof of lower waste, higher profit or customer impact. Commercial validation has not been performed.

Can very short-term price direction be predicted beyond a simple baseline — and does that signal survive execution costs?

≈60%Directional accuracy

What the result means: an experimental directional edge was observed at the 5-second horizon. It did not translate into economic viability under the evaluated exchange-fee assumptions. This is not a claim of profitability or financial advantage.

Approach
Short-horizon BTC/USDT directional classification with a Transformer, BiLSTM and Cross-Attention architecture; temporal evaluation and execution simulation.
Validation
Walk-forward checks, block bootstrap, paired gap analysis and permutation testing are documented in the project audit.
Limitations
Checkpoint selection and execution assumptions constrain interpretation. Queue position, partial fills and adverse selection were not fully modeled.

03 / VALIDATION ENGINEERING

A result is only
the beginning.

A single test metric does not establish generalization, reliability or practical usefulness. The validation process asks whether the result holds up over time, how uncertain it is, and whether it matters outside the metric.

STAGE 01 / 05 METHOD OVERVIEW

Start with a comparison.

A model score is hard to interpret in isolation. Compare it with a clearly defined baseline on aligned evaluation data before describing an improvement.

A better score is an observation — not, by itself, proof of generalization or business value.

THE PRINCIPLE

These methods help investigate specific failure modes. They do not automatically eliminate all bias or guarantee that a model will generalize.
